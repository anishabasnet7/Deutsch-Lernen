Group 2 — Coordinating conjunctions → NO word order change

These join two main clauses. Word order in the second clause stays completely normal.
WordMeaningExample
undandIch koche und er spült.
aberbutIch will, aber ich kann nicht.
oderorKommst du, oder bleibst du?

denn vs weil: Both mean "because." denn = normal order, can never start a sentence. weil = verb to end, can start.
aber vs sondern: Use sondern only after a negation (nicht/kein) when correcting: "not X, but rather Y."

Group 4 — Prepositions as connectors (noun phrase only)

These connect a noun phrase, NOT a full clause. They do not affect verb position.

wegen/trotz = genitive (formal) or dative (spoken): wegen dem Wetter ✓ in spoken German

Separable Verb Prefixes — Meanings & Examples A2

Separable prefixes split from the verb in main clauses: Ich mache die Tür auf. In infinitive form they stay together: aufmachen. In subordinate clauses they stay together: …weil er aufmacht.

aus- = off, out, finished

Key Verb Rules — war, wird, will, sein, werden, konnte A2

war / waren — past of sein (was / were)

war/waren = Präteritum (simple past) of sein. Used even in spoken German!
PronounFormExample
ichwarIch war gestern in Berlin. I was in Berlin yesterday.
duwarstWarst du krank? Were you sick?
er/sie/eswarDas war toll! That was great!
wirwarenWir waren im Urlaub. We were on holiday.
ihrwartWart ihr dabei? Were you there?
sie/SiewarenSie waren sehr nett. They were very nice.
Do NOT say Ich bin gestern in Berlin gewesen — use war directly. Both exist but war is more natural in speech.

wird / werden — becoming & future

werden has two main uses: (1) to BECOME something, (2) as a FUTURE auxiliary (+ infinitive at end).
Meaning 1: BECOMEMeaning 2: FUTURE (+ infinitive)
ich werdeIch werde Arzt. I'm becoming a doctor.Ich werde morgen lernen. I will study tomorrow.
du wirstDu wirst größer. You're getting taller.Du wirst es schaffen. You will make it.
er/sie wirdEs wird kalt. It's getting cold.Er wird kommen. He will come.
wir werdenWir werden alt.Wir werden gewinnen.
In everyday spoken German, present tense + time word replaces Futur I:
Ich lerne morgen. = Ich werde morgen lernen. (both correct)

will — present of wollen (NOT future!)

⚠️ will in German = "wants to" — it is NOT a future tense! This is the biggest false-friend mistake.
PronounFormExample
ichwillIch will Deutsch lernen. I want to learn German.
duwillstWillst du mitkommen? Do you want to come along?
er/sie/eswillEr will schlafen. He wants to sleep.
wir/sie/SiewollenWir wollen essen gehen. We want to go eat.
English "I will" ≠ German "ich will"
"I will come tomorrow" = Ich werde morgen kommen. OR Ich komme morgen.
"I want to come" = Ich will kommen.

konnte / konnten — past of können (could)

PronounFormExample
ichkonnteIch konnte nicht schlafen. I couldn't sleep.
dukonntestKonntest du ihn sehen? Could you see him?
er/sie/eskonnteSie konnte nicht kommen. She couldn't come.
wir/sie/SiekonntenWir konnten es nicht verstehen. We couldn't understand it.
Other modal past forms: musste (had to), wollte (wanted to), durfte (was allowed to), sollte (was supposed to)

sein vs haben in Perfekt — movement & change vs action

haben → most verbs
Transitive verbs (with direct object)
Reflexive verbs
Verbs that stay in one place
Ich habe gegessen. (ate)
Er hat gearbeitet. (worked)
Wir haben gespielt. (played)
Sie hat sich gewaschen. (washed herself)
sein → movement & change of state
Verbs of going from A to B
Change of condition/state
sein + bleiben + werden themselves
Ich bin gegangen. (went)
Er ist gefahren. (drove)
Sie ist aufgewacht. (woke up)
Es ist passiert. (happened)
Test: Can the verb have a direct object? If yes → probably haben. Is it about moving from place A to B, or changing state? → probably sein.

was für — what kind of / what a

was für = "what kind of" or "what a". The article after it follows normal case rules.
UseExample

ExclamationWas für ein schönes Haus! What a beautiful house!
With accusativeWas für einen Film siehst du? What kind of film are you watching?
ein/eine/ein after was für changes with gender and case — just like normal articles.

German School System A1A2

The German school system varies slightly by state (Bundesland), but the main structure is consistent across Germany.

School levels — from youngest to oldest

Age 0–6

Daycare / Preschool — not compulsory. Focus on play and development.
Age 6–10
Klasse 1–4

Primary school — compulsory for all children. Teaches reading, writing, arithmetic.
Age 10–15/16

Lower secondary school. Practical focus. Leads to vocational training (Ausbildung).
Age 10–16
Klasse 5–10

Intermediate secondary school. Mix of academic and practical. Opens doors to many vocational and some academic paths.
Age 10–18/19
Klasse 5–12/13

Academic secondary school. The most demanding track. Ends with Abitur (university entrance qualification).
Age 10–16
Klasse 5–10

Comprehensive school — combines all three tracks. Students can achieve any qualification level.
Age 16+
die Berufsschule / die Ausbildung
Vocational school + apprenticeship. Dual system: learn at school + work at a company (3 years).
Age 18+
die Universität / die Hochschule
University. Requires Abitur. Bachelor → Master → Doktor.
Age 18+

University of applied sciences. More practical focus. Requires Fachabitur or Abitur.
